3. Product and ownership model

One authenticated userSame email, password, session and user ID.
Personal WorkspaceOwn plans, Calendar, workouts, history, goals and progress.
Optional Coach WorkspaceTemplates, connected trainees, packages and authorized management.

account_type = trainee means personal access. account_type = coach adds coaching capability; it does not remove personal access. A coach can be another coach's trainee. Multiple coaches may connect to one person; self-coaching is rejected.

Keep the same Laravel/Inertia/React application and database. A coach template belongs to its coach. An assigned copy, its days, its Calendar entries and all recorded performance belong to the trainee. No impersonation, second plan engine, second progress engine, organizations, admin workspace or role-management framework.

Account capability controls whether coach routes are available. An accepted relationship controls read access. An effective subscription plus assignment/source ownership controls management. Workspace navigation alone never grants permission.

4. Proposed defaults for gaps in the draft

These are explicit implementation recommendations, not claims that the draft already approved every detail. They make the brief executable without leaving security or ownership decisions to individual screens.

  1. Owners keep control. Trainees may edit, duplicate and delete their own assigned plans through existing personal workflows. A personal duplicate is a personal plan without a coaching assignment. Coaches may edit their own active assignments or end them; they cannot delete the trainee-owned plan as an administrative shortcut.
  2. One open subscription per relationship. Treat active and paused packages as open, so a second package requires ending the existing one. This is slightly stricter than the draft's “one active” rule and avoids ambiguous resumes. Effective management additionally requires today's date to fall inside its service dates.
  3. Consent has a clear lifetime. Reconnecting after a relationship ends creates a new relationship row and requires fresh acceptance. It does not reactivate old subscriptions or assignments. Historical training is still visible after new consent, but old assignments do not silently regain management rights.
  4. Renewal within an uninterrupted relationship is different. A new effective subscription can permit management of that relationship's still-active assignments. An assignment's original subscription ID records where it came from; it is not the only subscription that may authorize future management.
  5. Coach scheduling stays within the service period. Only future dates inside the effective subscription are eligible for coach-created sessions. Show excluded dates in the preview; never silently shorten a plan. Existing sessions outside the period remain intact. Personal scheduling remains available to the trainee.
  6. Read consent is broad but explicit. An accepted coach can view the trainee's training goals, body-weight progress, Calendar, workout history and performance, including personal workouts and sessions from other coaches. Full editable plan browsing is limited to the coach's own assigned plans; personal and other-coach plan contents are visible only where already part of authorized session/history detail. Acceptance must explain that scope.
  7. No automatic external notifications. Requests appear in My Coaching and in-app counts. Email, push, chat, calls, “Join” and “Message” controls from the mockups are out of scope.
  8. Template assignment creates an unscheduled copy. Confirm trainee and assignment dates, then offer the existing schedule preview as a separate action. Assigning a plan never silently adds Calendar sessions.

The relationship FK on assignments is a necessary refinement to the draft: coach/trainee IDs alone cannot distinguish revoked consent from a later reconnection. Indexes and backfill

The actual database already has session_exercises_source_exercise_key_index, a unique schedule_entries.plan_occurrence_key index, schedule_entries(user_id, scheduled_date), and relevant training/session foreign-key indexes. Preserve these.

New lookup constraints are unique training code, unique coach-profile user, unique token hash and unique assigned-plan reference. Relationship queries need coach/status and trainee/status access, subscriptions need relationship/status, assignments need relationship/status, and packages need coach/active access. Inspect generated FK indexes before adding composites; use representative EXPLAIN results and avoid duplicate single-column indexes. Start with existing plan-owner and schedule-owner/date indexes before adding speculative workspace/attribution composites.

Add the training code as nullable with a unique index, backfill only missing values in chunks with collision retries, verify every account has a code, then enforce not-null. The deployment gate prevents new application registrations during this step. Preserve passwords, APP_KEY, user IDs, existing timestamps where practical, plans, logs and uploaded files. No reseeding, table recreation or core ownership migration.

6. Authorization contract

Use a focused CoachAccess service and explicit coach abilities. Every controller rechecks the authenticated actor, target trainee, current relationship, effective subscription where required, and resource ownership. A nullable or missing assignment/source fails closed. Do not cache an authorization decision in the login session.

7. Registration, upgrade and workspace switching

  1. Keep the existing login route, password hashing, session regeneration, CSRF protection and login rate limits. Registration adds “I'm training” and “I'm a coach” paths within the existing auth layout.
  2. Trainee registration creates a trainee account, training code and normal personal onboarding. Coach registration atomically creates a coach account, code and CoachProfile, then opens Coach Overview.
  3. Profile offers Enable Coach Workspace with display name, optional specialization and bio. Repeated submission is idempotent. Existing personal data remains untouched. No downgrade flow.
  4. Use the existing avatar menu for personal/coach switching. Route prefixes determine each page's actual context, so two tabs can safely show different workspaces. A validated session preference may control the next default landing page; it must not control query ownership.
  5. Personal onboarding is offered on the personal dashboard when needed. Coach navigation is never blocked by missing personal height, weight or goals.
  6. On login, honor a validated same-origin intended coach-connect URL before default onboarding redirection. A trainee who opens a coach-only link receives an explanation and optional coach setup, not automatic privilege elevation.

Personal navigation retains Overview, Training plans, Calendar, Training History, Progress and Profile, with My Coaching added. Coach navigation starts with Overview, Trainees, Coach Plans and Subscriptions; Calendar, History and Progress are contextual trainee tabs. The broad duplicate navigation shown in one mockup is unnecessary for this release.

Coach templates are excluded from all personal plan lists, schedule pickers, recommendation destinations, copy destinations and progression-template selections. Personal routes must also reject a manually supplied coach-template ID where it would enable personal scheduling or execution.

8. IDs, requests and QR

Every account displays its permanent Training Planner ID in Profile/My Coaching with Copy ID and Show Coach QR. Exact-ID lookup normalizes case and whitespace, validates the entire format, and returns only a limited display name and generic member label. No global email, phone, prefix or full-name search. Connected-trainee search may use name and code within the authorized relationship list.

Sending a request creates a pending relationship. The trainee sees the coach's display name, specialization and the explicit data-access scope before accepting. Pending requests grant no training access. Decline ends the request with a reason; the coach cannot self-approve. Duplicate requests return the current request, not another pending row.

QR lifecycle

  1. Authenticated trainee generates a cryptographically random 256-bit token; store its SHA-256 hash and a ten-minute expiry. Regeneration invalidates that trainee's older unused token. Do not place tokens in public storage or analytics.
  2. Render a local QR containing an HTTPS application URL. Do not send it to an external QR-generation service. No in-app camera scanner; use the phone camera.
  3. GET resolves the connection page or login return path. It never consumes the token, authenticates as the trainee, or sends a request. This avoids link previews and browser prefetch using it up.
  4. Authenticated coach explicitly submits Send Request with CSRF protection. In one transaction lock and validate the unused, unexpired, user-bound token; reject self-coaching; create/reuse the pending relationship; mark the token used.
  5. Acceptance is still required. Replays, expired tokens and simultaneous submissions cannot create another request or grant access.

Use private/no-store responses and a restrictive referrer policy on token pages, no third-party assets, and redaction of raw tokens from application logs. A QR URL is a short-lived invitation secret, never an authentication credential. Show a copyable temporary link as an accessible fallback.

Use Laravel per-account and per-IP rate limiting for exact lookup, request creation, token generation and token resolution. Initial caps can be 10 lookups/minute, 5 requests/minute plus 30/day, 5 QR generations/minute and 20 token resolutions/minute; keep these in one config location. Unknown/expired tokens get a generic unavailable response. The ID endpoint intentionally returns only the minimum lookup information.

Disconnect or End Relationship requires confirmation and immediately prevents subsequent coach reads/writes. End its open subscriptions and active assignments in the same transaction, retaining their records. No Calendar cleanup, plan deletion or history rewrite. Information already seen by a coach cannot be recalled; the app must revoke future requests, including stale open forms.

9. Service subscriptions

Coaches create reusable packages such as “8 Week Coaching,” 56 days. Creating a subscription snapshots the package name and chosen dates. Inclusive calculation is ends_on = starts_on + duration_days - 1. A custom date or open-ended service can be explicitly selected; an open-ended service still requires finite plan dates for schedule generation.

Management requires relationship active, subscription status active, starts_on <= today, and ends_on IS NULL OR ends_on >= today, using the application's existing timezone. Future-start and expired subscriptions display those derived states and grant read-only access. Do not rely on a nightly job to revoke permissions.

Pause disables management immediately. Resume rechecks dates and the single-open-subscription rule. End retains the record and plan/session data. Expiry does not delete sessions or stop the trainee completing their own workouts. Package edits affect only future subscriptions. Keep coach administrative notes private; share package, status and dates with the trainee.

No prices, cards, invoices, billing integration, renewal charges or payment status. There is no entitlement granted solely by a frontend “Active” badge.

10. Coach plans, assignment and private exercises

Reuse TrainingPlan → TrainingDay → TrainingExercise. Coach templates are coach-owned and appear only in Coach Plans. They retain weekly patterns, usual times, prescriptions, icons, thumbnails and supersets; they do not generate the coach's personal Calendar. Assignment asks for a trainee, effective subscription and finite plan period.

Extend TrainingPlanDuplicator with a dedicated assignment operation that takes the real actor and authorized trainee. Reuse its existing day/exercise/media copying primitive and rollback cleanup. In one transaction create the trainee-owned coach_assigned plan and its assignment record. Preserve exercise order, sets, reps/duration, target weights, tracking modes, library keys, supersets and independently copied media.

No sessions, snapshots, actuals, workout history or PRs are copied. Editing or deleting the template later cannot alter the copy. Creating a custom plan for one trainee uses the existing builder and creates the same assignment record with a null source template. Ending an assignment preserves the plan and Calendar but removes coach management rights.

Show “Coach assigned · [coach display name]” on personal plan cards/details. The owner can still train, edit, duplicate or delete their own plan. Coach Apply or plan edits never mutate another assignment, even when both copies share an exercise identity. A coach's Duplicate Template stays a template; an assignment always goes through Assign Plan. Copy-to-plan destinations must be authorized in the current context, not every plan owned by the trainee.

Custom library contract

The bundled library is shared. Private custom libraries remain private. During a coach edit, newly selected private exercises come from that coach's library; during a trainee edit, they come from the trainee's library. The authorized writer copies the chosen media into the destination exercise, independent of the source.

An already-saved exercise may retain its unchanged inherited custom:{id} key even if the current actor cannot browse the original library or the original item is later removed. Validate that exception against the existing authorized exercise row, not a client-supplied key alone. New or changed keys must resolve in an allowed source library. Do not allow arbitrary custom IDs, storage paths or uploaded media belonging to strangers.

Preserve the stable identity key on assignment; do not remap it to a new private-library row and split historical progress. Editing the assigned exercise's description/prescription/media does not edit the original library record. “Save to my library” creates only actor-owned custom content under existing rules; no full library is copied or shared automatically.

Refactor the day writer and library validation together. Updating only the UI or duplicator would leave the assigned custom exercise failing validation when the trainee saves the day. Explicitly test that save, source deletion, disconnect and media independence.

11. Reuse the v1.5 schedule manager

Keep PlanSchedule as the only finite schedule-diff engine. Its current preview queries the plan owner's entries, reserves original occurrence keys even after moves, and produces ADD / KEEP / REMOVE / PROTECTED. Add a coach management context with an authenticated actor and already-authorized assignment; do not replace its algorithm.

  1. Preview everything relevant. Continue loading all existing occurrences/bookings for the trainee's assigned plan. Personal or other-coach bookings must still block duplicate creation even when the current coach cannot modify them.
  2. Add only authorized dates. Plan dates, weekday/time rules, strict future cutoff and service dates determine available additions. Show outside-service dates separately with a reason. New entries use trainee user_id, coach attribution, the existing occurrence key and schedule_managed = true for generated entries.
  3. Restrict removal/replacement. The row must pass every v1.5 protection and additionally belong to this trainee, be attributed to this coach and resolve to this coach's active assignment in the current consent cycle. Unknown origin, missing/deleted assignment, null attribution or another actor's session is protected.
  4. Keep existing protections. Completed/skipped status, started workouts, snapshots, structured rows, historical dates, manually scheduled entries, independent edits and moved occurrences remain protected from bulk actions. Today without a time is historical, matching existing History behavior. The one-year/1,000-occurrence limits remain.
  5. Confirm explicitly. Add Missing does not replace times. Regenerate removes obsolete eligible sessions only when selected. Remove Future shows the entire affected list. No Calendar change occurs merely because plan dates, weekdays, times or prescriptions changed.

Extend the signed preview context to include actor, trainee, assignment, consent cycle and effective subscription state/dates. Recheck under locks at submission. A pause, disconnect, source change, owner edit, workout start or date-boundary change invalidates a stale request. Personal preview tokens cannot be reused as coach tokens.

Single-session scheduling reuses the existing form and ScheduleEntry model, selecting a day only from this coach's active assignment. It writes a planned trainee-owned entry with attribution. A manually created entry remains protected from bulk regeneration. Explicit individual coach edits/deletion may affect only this coach's own future, unstarted, unsaved entry with a live assignment and valid service dates; never another coach's/personal entry or any actual-performance field. Individual Calendar edits retain attribution and keep the existing bulk opt-out behavior.

When a day/assignment link no longer resolves, fail closed for coach management; the trainee retains their existing owner controls. No additional session-assignment FK is required for v1.6 because current plan ownership plus assignment and attribution establish authority; an unresolved link grants no access. Do not infer a manager from a matching title.

Show a small “Scheduled by” label on Session Details. Do not flood the Calendar with badges. The Friday/Saturday weekend shortcuts and all personal scheduling behavior remain unchanged.

12. Monitoring without another reporting engine

The Coach Overview shows active relationships, today's trainee sessions, pending outgoing requests and upcoming sessions. Count people separately from sessions and label training/rest totals clearly. Upcoming lists and recent activity use authorized trainees only, with bounded lists and the existing scrolling-card treatment.

Use current completed/skipped sessions, relationship/subscription timestamps and existing PR calculations for lightweight recent activity. This is a current-state summary, not a durable event log. Do not add an activity-stream table or fabricate the time a PR was achieved from unrelated timestamps.

Trainee details use Overview, Plan, Calendar, Training History and Progress tabs. Overview includes goals, current package, this coach's active assignment(s), next session, recent workout, body summary, recent records and completion rate. Reuse the existing monthly due-training completion calculation and label its period; do not copy the mockup's “last four weeks” label onto a different calculation.

  • History: extract the existing filter/query/summary into a small shared query service accepting an explicit owner. Preserve 10 items by default, 25/50/100/all, actual date-period behavior, session-type/status/plan filters and snapshot-based historical plan identity.
  • Calendar: share the existing month/week/list query/rendering, passing the trainee and coach route URLs. Read visibility does not imply permission to schedule or edit every visible session.
  • Session detail: reuse ScheduleEntry::workoutDetails(), actual-set rendering and ExerciseProgress::forSession(). Omit all status, start/finish/discard, actual-weight, calorie and duration editing actions in coach context.
  • Progress: call BodyWeightHistory and ExerciseProgress with the authorized trainee. Preserve current-completed-only inclusion, source identity/tracking mode, date ranges, charts and PR definitions.
  • Weighted metrics: only actual weights greater than zero qualify for weighted PRs/volume/charts. Zero/null weight may still count as reps or duration; never fabricate a zero-kg record.

Shared components need capability props and context-specific links. ExerciseProgress::url(), session record links, pagination, history filter forms and the progression selector currently point to personal paths; parameterize presentation links without changing metric calculations. Tests must confirm a coach's click stays on the intended trainee and never shows the coach's own progress.

Before acceptance expose no profile, body weight, history or metrics. After acceptance expose only the fields necessary for the stated training consent; email, birthdate, login details and unrelated coaching administrative notes are not trainee-monitoring props. Existing public exercise media is not an access-control mechanism: do not store QR tokens or sensitive coaching documents on that disk.

13. v1.4 progression stays the same calculation

The existing ExerciseProgression service already scopes evidence to the subject user and selected training-exercise template, evaluates the latest two current completed occurrences before qualifying sets, and protects Apply with a signed fingerprint and locking reads. Preserve those behaviors exactly.

Add a distinct coach Apply entry point using the real actor, trainee and assignment, sharing the existing evaluation and update core. Its selected template must belong to this coach's active assignment and trainee. Do not pass the trainee as an authenticated stand-in to bypass authorization. The coach identity remains unchanged throughout.

Read-only coach views may show suggestions for that coach's assigned plans, but no write token or Apply action when management is unavailable. A signed coach Apply token binds actor, trainee, current relationship/assignment/subscription and existing template/evidence fingerprint. Recheck all permissions in the transaction before changing only the selected planned weight or reps/duration field.

Changes to personal templates, another coach's assignment, ended assignments, expired/paused services or disconnected relationships are rejected. Deleting the template leaves historical performance read-only. No auto-Apply, automatic schedule regeneration, actual-log changes, cross-copy prescription updates or new progression algorithm.

14. Transactions, revocation and stale forms

New cross-user writes lock participant user rows in ascending ID order, then relationship, relevant subscription and assignment, followed by plan/day/exercise/session rows in a consistent order. This extends the existing owner and parent-session locks rather than introducing a distributed lock system.

  • Request creation and reconnection use the same participant locks to enforce one open directional relationship without a speculative generated-column uniqueness scheme.
  • Subscription start/resume and all management mutations lock the relationship and reread effective state. Pause/end/disconnect use those same locks, preventing a stale authorization check from committing later.
  • Schedule application locks the trainee owner, plan, days and relevant entries, then recomputes the signed preview using current reads. Workout start/log/status protection remains serialized by the existing user/session locks.
  • Assignment atomically writes the copied plan plus provenance. If any database or media operation fails, remove only the newly copied files and roll back the records.
  • QR request creation consumes the token atomically; page viewing does not. A repeated submission returns a safe existing-state result or an unavailable token, never duplicate authorization.
  • Recheck at the mutation boundary; neither a previous GET nor a visible button is sufficient. Authorization is checked on every read request as well. Already-rendered information is not retroactively erased.

Exercise identity and occurrence indexes are reused. No Redis, queues, WebSockets, Node server, cron-dependent permissions or generic event/version history. Use two database connections in targeted MySQL tests to prove the important races under REPEATABLE READ, rather than trusting sequential tests alone.

18. Required acceptance evidence

Use at least two coaches, two trainees and a coach who is also a trainee. Include shared builtin keys, private custom exercises, long names, active workouts, saved-but-planned sessions, expired packages and both personal and assigned plans. Never run destructive database tests against real local or production accounts.

  1. Account preservation: unique non-sequential code backfill, collision retry, repeated upgrade, no password/data changes, both registration flows, unchanged login/session protections, personal onboarding and QR login return.
  2. Workspace isolation: ordinary user denied every coach route; switching preserves identity; two tabs keep their contexts; templates never leak into personal plan/calendar/progression selectors or crafted personal requests.
  3. Connection states: duplicate requests, self-request, reversed coach/trainee pair, coach receiving coaching, accept/decline by correct person only, disconnect, reconnect with fresh consent and no revived old management.
  4. Discovery/QR: exact code only, no sensitive pre-consent fields, throttling, wrong/expired/used token, token bound to target, GET does not consume, logged-out return, no impersonation, simultaneous POSTs consume once.
  5. Subscriptions: owner-only packages, inclusive 56-day date calculation, package edits leave existing service unchanged, start/end boundaries, open-ended case, future start, expiry, pause/resume/end, no second open subscription under races.
  6. Assignment: trainee ownership, complete deep copy of training/rest days, stable identity/modes/supersets, independent media and rollback; source edits/deletion have no effect; copied private custom exercise remains editable; stranger custom keys rejected.
  7. Plan permissions: coach cannot edit personal/other-coach plans or select unauthorized copy destinations; trainee keeps owner controls; personal duplicate has no inherited management assignment; ending/deleting preserves workout history.
  8. Schedule permissions: correct trainee/actor attribution, service-date preview, manual/other-coach bookings still prevent duplicates; all v1.5 protections remain; missing assignment/null attribution fail closed; personal and coach preview tokens cannot cross contexts.
  9. Forbidden writes: send direct requests to start, finish, discard, complete/skip, actual weights/sets, calories, duration and body-weight endpoints as coach. Every attempt against the trainee is rejected, even with an active subscription.
  10. Read equivalence: authorized trainee History, Calendar, body progress, exercise charts, PRs and session detail match existing personal calculations; filters, default 10 items, pagination and links retain correct subject/context.
  11. Metric regression: zero/null actual weight still counts for reps/duration as applicable but never weighted PRs/volume; reopening/skipping removes a session from metrics; recompletion uses final saved structured data.
  12. Progression: same latest-occurrence/template rules, permitted coach Apply affects one planned field only; reject other-coach/personal templates, stale evidence, paused/expired/ended/disconnected access and token replay.
  13. Concurrency: two-connection MySQL tests for request uniqueness, subscription uniqueness, QR consumption, disconnect/pause racing plan/schedule/Apply, and workout start/completion racing schedule edits. Use current locking reads.
  14. Mobile/browser: 320px, 390px and desktop across both workspaces, QR, requests, subscriptions, assignment, long trainee lists, modals, Calendar and Progress. Check page/dialog overflow, keyboard access, loading/error/empty states and browser console.

Run PHP on SQLite and the isolated MySQL database sequentially because filesystem fakes share paths. Run JavaScript tests, production build, relevant formatting and whitespace checks. Full regression includes the existing 320 PHP / 24 JavaScript baseline; record actual new totals rather than predicting them. No tests were added for this documentation-only review.

19. Eventual release requirements

Target v1.6.0, retaining Hostinger compatibility. Do not deploy until all seven phases and the complete cross-user authorization model are finished. Use the established private backup, maintenance deployment, additive migration, cache rebuild, checksum and cleanup workflow. Include any QR dependency in the tested artifact; production must not require a Node process or queue worker.

Before migration record original account/training/upload fingerprints. Verify the expected baseline, database capabilities and actual indexes on the target. Back up database, files and environment privately; apply code/schema as a compatible set. Afterward, compare all original training columns and uploads, verify only expected account backfills/new fields changed, and confirm existing credentials and APP_KEY remain intact.

Run authenticated live tests with isolated temporary coach/trainee accounts, including authorization denials and preserved personal workflows; remove only those fixtures. Verify served assets, health/login, original accounts and removal of deployment helpers. Publish the immutable v1.6.0 tag only for the verified release commit.

Rollback must preserve newly recorded trainee data. During an immediately failed maintenance deployment, restore the private pre-release backup before reopening. After users have written v1.6 data, prefer a forward fix; do not blindly roll back to v1.5, which does not filter coach templates or understand new permissions, and do not drop coaching tables to make old code start.

The completion report must cover all 26 requested topics: summary; account model; registration; workspace behavior; upgrade; training IDs; QR security; relationships; subscriptions; coach-plan architecture; trainee ownership; schedule attribution; authorization matrix; disconnect; files added/changed; migrations; security tests; functional tests; mobile checks; PHP/JavaScript/build results; live verification; release notes; and remaining issues.

This review makes no production changes. v1.5.0 remains the deployed release. The next step is implementation of this reconciled scope when requested.
